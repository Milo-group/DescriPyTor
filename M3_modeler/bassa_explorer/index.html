How to read this page

  • Models. One column is one model BASSA visited; one row is one descriptor. Grey bar R², coloured bar Q² with those descriptors fixed (colour = number of descriptors), red dashes the nested Q² of the whole procedure. On the frequency bars: black dashes are what chance gives, and the grey band around them is how far that line could move with other shuffles; the thin dark line is the spread between independent chains; the blue line is the range over the reruns that each leave one compound out. Beside the bar: in how many of those reruns the descriptor stays among the top three, and that range in numbers. A wide blue line means the sampler's preference for the descriptor depends on single compounds, which is not the same as its effect depending on them.
  • An orange "!" beside a descriptor means one compound sits more than five standard deviations from all the others in that column. Such a column can carry a model on a single compound; check it before trusting the model.
  • Which descriptors go together. Green arcs join descriptors that share models more than chance; red dashed arcs join descriptors that stand in for each other. Select a descriptor and every other row is tinted green or red by how it behaves beside it. The stripe at the left marks a family of correlated descriptors.
  • Recipes reduce every model to its families: a card is one combination, and inside each slot are the descriptors that fill it, most used first. Descriptors in one slot replace each other.
  • Network and Map show the same relations as a picture: nodes or squares, green for together and red for instead.
  • Compounds shows, for the ten most used descriptors, how their frequency moves when each compound is left out and the sampler rerun. A red column is a compound the result rests on.
  • Key models cuts the table to one column per idea: models that differ only by swapping a descriptor for another of its family are merged, and groups visited in less than 1 % of the run are hidden.
  • The plot predicts every compound from a fit that left it out. Click a point to look at the compound; shift-click to compare, and shift-click further points to add more (tick "stack, aligned" to superpose them in one picture, with the descriptors drawn on each); right-click to hold it out, so that the model is fitted without it and predicts it, like a test compound. Diamonds are external compounds, never fitted. Grey ticks are what the class mean alone predicts.
  • Under the table of the shown model, one line per descriptor shows where its frequency goes when each compound is left out: a dot far from the tick is a compound that descriptor rests on. After Rerun BASSA without them, blue marks and "was …" show every frequency before.
  • Threshold turns one descriptor into a yes/no rule: choose the response value that counts as a hit, and the page finds the single cut on the descriptor that separates hits best. Read the leave-one-out line, where the cut is chosen again without each compound. Below it, every descriptor is ranked as that single rule, and a button tests the best of them against shuffled hits; a second descriptor can be added to see whether two rules together separate what one cannot.
  • On the molecule, the checkboxes above the viewer add the B1 plane, the angle θ, the full Sterimol set or the end-on map to every axis drawn. Names in the plot labels all compounds, only the external ones, or none.
  • Design gives a slider per descriptor of the shown model. Pin keeps a model. New case… runs BASSA on your own table; Keep this case adds it to the menu.

A model picked by eye from this page has been selected on these compounds. Quote the nested Q², not the bar.

All cases

Leading descriptor: the one in most models, with its share, the range of that share when one compound is left out, and in how many of those reruns it stays among the top three. Against chance: p of that share under shuffled responses. Predicts: the nested Q² of the whole procedure. Click a row to open the case.

The links beside the descriptor names

Each arc joins two descriptors and says how they behave across all the models BASSA visited.

  • Green, solid: they appear together. The two are in the same model more often than two unrelated descriptors would be. They carry different information and BASSA wants both.
  • Red, dashed: they replace each other. The two columns are strongly correlated and are rarely in the same model. They carry the same information under two names; a model takes one or the other.
  • Thicker means a stronger effect. No arc means nothing either way.
  • Strongest draws only the six clearest arcs of each kind, so the column stays readable. Point at a descriptor, or select it, and all of its links appear. All draws everything; off hides them.
  • Click an arc to select the two descriptors it joins.

The same information is in the tint of the rows once a descriptor is selected, and as pictures under Recipes, Network and Map.

Rows by: three ways to order the descriptors

  • Frequency. The plain ranking. The descriptor BASSA used most is on top; the grey bar is the share of all visited models that contain it. Use it to see what matters at all.
  • Goes with selection. Click a descriptor first. The others are then ordered by how they behave beside it: at the top, the descriptors that appear in the same models much more often than usual (its partners, tinted green); at the bottom, the ones that almost never share a model with it (tinted red, usually because they say the same thing). The small bar in the "beside" column runs from the descriptor's usual share (the tick) to its share next to your selection. Use it to answer "what does BASSA combine this with?".
  • Families. A family is a group of descriptors whose columns are strongly correlated (0.7 or more with the most used one): different names for nearly the same information. This order puts each family together, marks it with a coloured stripe at the left, and draws a line between families. BASSA usually takes one member of a family per model, so a family is the unit to read, not the single descriptor: three descriptors at 20 % each in one family are one idea at about 60 %. Use it to see how many different ideas the models draw on.

Say what this column measures

New case from your own table

First column: the compound name. One file per compound in the folder, named like the compound (L1.feather, or a longer name that contains L1 as a word). Feathers and logs bring charges and the dipole; xyz brings the geometry only. Descriptors are drawn on the molecule when their column names follow the DescriPyTor platform (B1_6-4, loc_B5_6-4, nbo_atom_5, mu_y[C4->N5|C6], 0_angle_[5, 6, 7]); other columns are used everywhere else.
